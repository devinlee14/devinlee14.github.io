Heavy equipment spare parts procurement analytics

Project 1 of 5

Problem

A heavy-equipment contractor with 4 remote sites spent USD 51M on spare parts in two years (14 suppliers, 420 parts). Where does the money go, which suppliers can be trusted, what does urgent buying cost, and how much critical stock should be held?

Approach (SQL + Python)

  • Spend analysis and ABC classification: 23% of parts make up 80% of spend.
  • Supplier scorecard: OTIF, lead-time variability, reject rate and price vs contract.
  • Cost of urgent and off-contract buying by site; approval cycle time by PO value.
  • 3-way match (PO vs goods receipt vs invoice) and safety stock for 45 critical parts at 95% service.
~$522ksavings identified per year (2% of spend)
82.5%OTIF; aftermarket only 60–85%
3.3%invoices mismatched, $370k overbilling caught

Hotel utility data pipeline and ESG reporting

Project 2 of 5

Problem

40 hotels in 8 countries type monthly electricity, water and gas figures by hand. Errors such as MWh entered as kWh, shifted decimals, missing or duplicate months, and values that don't match the bill flow straight into reported emissions.

Approach

  • Standardised IDs, period formats and units; removed duplicates.
  • Reconciled every value against utility bills (±2%), including bills on offset cycles, and classified the cause of each mismatch.
  • Filled gaps from bills or flagged estimates; robust z-scores to catch implausible values.
  • Logged every issue and every change for audit; produced an invoice gap report and Scope 1 & 2 emissions.

E-commerce growth and customer retention

Project 3 of 5

Problem

Olist, a Brazilian marketplace, grew GMV almost threefold in a year. Where did that growth come from, and do customers come back?

Approach (SQL, DuckDB)

  • Monthly KPIs with window functions (LAG, rolling averages).
  • Cohort retention on the real customer ID (customer_unique_id), not the per-order ID.
  • RFM segmentation and category Pareto with running totals.
  • Repeat-purchase drivers, tested with a two-proportion z-test.

Key findings

  • GMV +181% (H1 2018 vs H1 2017); Black Friday lifted GMV 53% in one month.
  • Only 3.0% of customers ever order twice; growth is almost entirely new customers.
  • A late first delivery cuts the repeat rate by 22% (p = 0.002).

Delivery experience root cause analysis

Project 4 of 5

Problem

How much does late delivery drive 1–2 star reviews, and which part of fulfilment should operations fix first?

Approach

  • Split each order into approval, seller handling and carrier transit.
  • Logistic regression controlling for lead time, items, multi-seller orders, distance, freight and price.
  • Late rate by month, state and seller; impact sizing of excess bad reviews.
13.9×odds of a 1–2 star review when late

Recommendation

Plan carrier capacity before Black Friday and Q1, alert on missed seller handovers, and contact customers once an order passes its estimated date.
