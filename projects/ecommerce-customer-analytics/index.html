E-commerce growth and customer retention

Olist grew GMV almost threefold in a year. I used SQL to check where that growth came from, and found a marketplace that is excellent at acquiring customers and almost never sees them again.

Growth is real, and seasonal

Monthly GMV went from R$137k in January 2017 to around R$1M through 2018. Black Friday (November 2017) lifted GMV 53% in a single month, the biggest spike in the data.

Questions I answered in SQL:

  • How fast is GMV growing, and what drives the peaks?
  • Do customers come back after their first order?
  • Which segments hold the revenue?
  • Does the first-order experience change repeat behaviour?

Almost nobody comes back

A key detail: Olist gives a new customer_id to every order. Joining on customer_unique_id instead reveals the real person. Without that fix, retention can't be measured at all.

Every monthly cohort retains under 1% of customers in any later month, and only 3.0% of all customers ever order twice. Growth is coming almost entirely from new customers.

02_cohort_retention.sqlFull file on GitHub
WITH first_purchase AS (
    SELECT customer_id, order_month,
           MIN(order_month) OVER (PARTITION BY customer_id) AS cohort_month
    FROM orders
),
activity AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT customer_id, cohort_month,
           DATE_DIFF('month', cohort_month, order_month) AS month_number
    FROM first_purchase
),
cohort_size AS (
    SELECT cohort_month, COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS customers
    FROM activity WHERE month_number = 0 GROUP BY cohort_month
)
SELECT STRFTIME(a.cohort_month, '%Y-%m') AS cohort,
       s.customers,
       a.month_number,
       COUNT(DISTINCT a.customer_id)                              AS active,
       ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(DISTINCT a.customer_id) / s.customers, 2) AS retention_pct
FROM activity a
JOIN cohort_size s USING (cohort_month)
WHERE a.month_number <= 12
GROUP BY a.cohort_month, s.customers, a.month_number
ORDER BY a.cohort_month, a.month_number;

The first delivery shapes loyalty

Taking only each customer's first order (with at least 6 months to return), customers whose first delivery arrived late came back 22% less often: 3.19% vs 4.09%.

A two-proportion z-test puts the chance of seeing this gap by luck at p = 0.002. A 5-star first review also goes with a higher repeat rate than a 1-star one.

That's why I followed up with a separate root cause analysis of late deliveries.

Recommendations

  • Build a second-purchase programme

    Target "New high-value" customers 30–45 days after delivery. Moving the repeat rate from 3% to 5% means about 1,900 more returning customers per 95k acquired.

  • Win back lost high-value customers

    13.9k people averaging R$309 each. Use category-specific campaigns based on what they bought first.

  • Treat on-time delivery as a retention lever

    Late first orders measurably reduce repeat purchases, so logistics KPIs belong in the growth review, not only the ops review.

  • Plan for Black Friday early

    The biggest demand spike of the year. Inventory, carrier capacity and customer service need to scale ahead of it.
